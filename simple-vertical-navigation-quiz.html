Simple model: treat IAS as true airspeed (TAS). Airspeed, wind, and climb or descent rate remain constant. Wind is directly along track; ignore crosswind and the effect of climb angle on horizontal speed.

Add tailwind to airspeed, subtract headwind; calm leaves it unchanged. Distance is over the ground. Initial and final altitudes use the same datum.

ft

Mechanical-computer leeway: ±50 ft. Decimal feet are welcome.

How the answers are calculated

GS (kt) = IAS + tailwind, or IAS - headwind, under this quiz's IAS = TAS assumption.

Time (minutes) = ground distance (nm) × 60 / GS (kt).

Altitude change (ft) = vertical speed (ft/min) × time (minutes). Use positive vertical speed for a climb and negative for a descent.

Final altitude = initial altitude + altitude change. Keep full precision until the final answer.

The reference uses IAS 75 kt, tailwind 20 kt, climb rate 500 ft/min, and ground distance 5 nm. GS is 95 kt; time is 300 / 95 = 3.1579... minutes, giving 500 × 300 / 95 = 1578.9474... ft gained. A CRP-1 reading of 1,575 ft passes the stated tolerance.

Random exercises: IAS 50–150 kt, head/tailwind 5–30 kt or calm, vertical rates 250–750 ft/min, distances 1–10 nm. Altitude changes are limited to 4,000 ft; final-altitude scenarios stay between 500 and 10,000 ft. Simplified practice only; not for operational navigation.
